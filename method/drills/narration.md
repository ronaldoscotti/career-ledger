# narration

An answer that reads well is not yet an answer the person can say. Most preparation is lost in that distance, because the writing looks finished and nobody tests the other half.

This file is the exercise. `method/funnel/_refs/spoken-gate.md` holds the rules the text has to satisfy before the exercise is worth running. Run the gate first, then run this.

## The loop

Four steps, one answer at a time. It takes about four minutes per answer.

1. **Start a timer and say the answer out loud**, at the speed the person would use in a room, to you or to nobody. Reading it silently does not count and neither does mumbling it.
2. **Mark where it broke.** A place they ran out of breath, a word they swapped, a sentence they restarted, a number they had to think about. Mark the exact sentence and move on without discussing it.
3. **Rewrite the marked sentences.** Not the whole answer. The sentence that broke, and the one before it when the break was caused by the run-up.
4. **Say it again, timed.** Stop when nothing new breaks and the time is inside the target.

Do not let the person practise a sentence until it comes out. That trains a recital, and a recital collapses the first time an interviewer interrupts it.

## The timing targets

| Kind of answer | Target | Over when |
|---|---|---|
| Who they are, at the top of a call | Ninety seconds | It reaches the present day instead of reaching this room |
| A behavioral story | Two minutes | The situation has taken more than twenty seconds |
| A technical or design answer | Three minutes, with pauses for the interviewer | It has no pause in it at all |
| Why this company | Sixty seconds | It could be said about a competitor |
| A question the person asks | Fifteen seconds | It needs a preamble to make sense |

## What to cut first when one runs long

In this order, and the order is what makes it fast.

1. **The setup.** The situation is almost always twice the length it needs. Two sentences, then the person appears.
2. **The second example.** An answer carrying two examples is an answer where neither one lands, and the person added the second because the first felt thin.
3. **The names of things nobody in the room knows.** Internal system names, team names, product names. Replace with what the thing did.
4. **The qualifiers.** Every "sort of", "I guess", "kind of" and "a little bit". They cost time and they cost the claim.

Cut the conclusion last. An answer that ends on its number ends well, and the number is the part people drop first because it feels like boasting.

## A sentence that breaks twice is a defect in the text

When the same sentence breaks on two separate runs, stop rehearsing and send it back to the writer. Rewrite it, in whatever file it came from, and run `method/funnel/_refs/spoken-gate.md` over the replacement.

The stumble is information about the sentence and never about the person. Treating it as a performance problem produces somebody who has practised a sentence they still cannot say, which is worse than the original, because now they are also tense about it.

## When the interview is in a second language

Rehearse in the language of the interview. A story rehearsed in one language and delivered in another arrives translated, and the translation happens live, which costs the composure that belonged to the answer.

Keep a running list of the words that vanish under pressure. The word that disappears is rarely the difficult one. It is an ordinary word the person knows perfectly well and cannot reach while also thinking about what they are saying.

That list goes in the dossier, short, as words rather than sentences. Reading it once before the call is enough to put the words back within reach. Do not build it into a script.

## What this drill does not cover

It does not test whether the answer is true, whether it holds up under a follow-up, or whether it answers what was asked. Those belong to `method/drills/mock-interview.md`, which runs an interviewer against the answers instead of a clock.
