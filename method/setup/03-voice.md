# Setup step 03, the voice profile

Around fifteen minutes. Three samples in, one file out: `profile/voice.md`.

`method/voice.md` is subtractive. It strips what is not the person. This step produces the other half, which says what is. Without it the gate still works, and everything that survives it reads correct and anonymous.

## What to ask for

Three pieces of the person's own writing, unedited, at least a few paragraphs each, from moments when they were not performing.

- A message to a colleague explaining something.
- Something they wrote to justify a decision, in a document, a review or a thread.
- Anything they wrote only for themselves.

Two kinds of sample are worse than none. A cover letter is already written in the register this gate exists to remove. Anything an assistant helped with is contaminated by the thing being measured. Ask directly whether either applies, because people offer their best writing and their best writing is usually the least like them.

If they can only find two, work with two and record that in the file. Three is a target, not a gate.

## What to extract

Read the three together and look for what repeats. One sample shows a mood. Three show a habit.

| Dimension | What to record |
|---|---|
| Sentence length | The typical length, and how much it varies inside a paragraph |
| Order | Whether they open with the conclusion or build toward it |
| Vocabulary | Words and constructions they reach for more than once, especially ordinary ones |
| Formality | The default level, and what makes it shift |
| Numbers | Whether they quantify by habit, round, or avoid figures |
| Disagreement | How they signal it, since most people have one move and use it every time |
| Absences | What they never do. This carries more weight than any of the rows above |

Record nothing that only one sample supports, and nothing the other two contradict.

## What not to record

Typos. Tics they would not defend if shown. A construction that appears once. Anything that reads like a compliment, because a voice profile is a description and a description that flatters stops being usable.

## The output

Short and declarative, one observation per line, so `method/voice.md` can read it without interpretation. Close the file with two lines of the person's actual writing, quoted, as a reference sample. A reader comparing a draft against the profile needs something to hear.

## The disagreement this step will produce, and how it resolves

This step describes how the person writes today. It is not an instruction to keep them there.

Where a recorded habit and `method/voice.md` conflict, the gate wins. Somebody whose habit is a long subordinate clause still ships the short version, because the reader on the other end is skimming.

Record the habit anyway. A profile that quietly drops every habit the gate would reject is a profile that agrees with the gate about everything, which makes it worth nothing. The disagreement is the useful part, and it belongs on the page where a person can see it and decide.

## When to run it again

Any time the person's writing changes enough that they notice, and after any stretch where they wrote a lot in a second language. The file is cheap to rebuild and expensive to leave stale.
