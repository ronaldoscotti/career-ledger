# interview

This file runs twice for every stage. Once before, to prepare it. Once after, in the post-call, to capture what happened while it is still recoverable.

The output of the preparation is two files, the dossier and the prompter, described in `method/funnel/_refs/delivery.md`.

## The axiom

The bank is constant. Only the mapping changes.

Every interview method is a different projection of the same stories from `profile/experience/`. A preparation that finds itself writing a story the bank does not contain has hit one of two situations, and `method/funnel/_refs/star.md` names both: the bank is incomplete and the fact gets collected under ZERO-B first, or the preparation is inventing and the session stops there.

## The ruler for the whole preparation

The person already passed the competence bar. Nobody schedules a call believing the person in it cannot do the job. The resume answered that question, which is why the call exists at all.

What the room measures from here is alignment. Whether this person fits this seat, this team and this problem, and whether the seniority they present matches the seniority being bought.

This opens the file because the default behaviour of somebody who undervalues themselves is to spend the whole call proving they can do the work. That optimises for a question nobody is still asking, and it spends the time that belonged to the question actually on the table. Check every prepared answer against this ruler before it goes in the prompter.

## How the stage runs, and where it stops

Read the sources. **Stop and ask.** Build the map and the three selling points. **Stop and ask again.** Only then write prose.

A preparation is written with the person, not about them. On 2026-08-02 a stage preparation was rewritten four times, and every correction came from a fact or a judgment only the person had. All four arrived after the prose was finished, when changing anything meant changing everything. The two checkpoints exist so those facts arrive while they are still cheap.

**The first checkpoint, after reading the sources and before mapping.** Ask what the files cannot answer, as a short numbered list. Never a generic offer to take questions, because a generic offer gets a generic answer. The list covers what the invitation actually said, what has changed since the application went out, any factual matter the preparation will need and cannot look up, and what the person wants to land in this specific call.

**The second checkpoint, after the map and the selling points, before any prose exists.** Show the map. Ask three things. Which rows marked as matching are actually thin, since the person knows which of their own stories they would not want probed. Which gaps they can close with a story the bank does not have yet, which is a ZERO-B collection and not an improvisation. And whether the three selling points are the right three. Changing the spine after the prose is written means rewriting the prose, which is exactly what the incident above cost.

## A marker never survives into a delivered artifact

A placeholder in a dossier is a hidden question, and a hidden question does not get asked. The person reads past it, or reads it as something already handled.

When an answer did not come, the artifact carries a stated assumption and the pending item is repeated out loud at delivery. An assumption the person can see gets corrected in five seconds. A bracket they skim past goes into the room with them.

## The sources, in order

1. **The posting, read in full.** Not skimmed. It is the document the stage measures against, and the requirement that gets asked about is frequently the one buried in the third paragraph.
2. **What was actually sent.** The derived resume and any letter, from `applications/<company>/`. The interviewer read this, and it is the only thing in the room that both sides have.
3. **The frozen `fit` field** on the card. It carries the judgment of the day the posting was read, before weeks of process reshaped everyone's memory of why this looked interesting.
4. **`profile/experience/`**, with `profile/experience/locks.md` for the numbers and `profile/experience/conflicts.md` for the ones that are not settled.
5. **The public profile of whoever is running the stage.** Only what they chose to publish. `method/funnel/_refs/reputation.md` governs how it is used, and the rule is that a fact about a person becomes a question and never a recitation.
6. **The four company questions** from `method/funnel/_refs/reputation.md`, including the pattern rule over the reviews.
7. **Feedback from the previous stage.** The highest-value source that exists, and the one most often skipped because it lives in a note rather than a file. Read it before anything else when there is one.

## Find the shape of the process instead of guessing it

Work down this list and stop at the first thing that answers.

1. **Ask the recruiter.** Expected, normal, and almost nobody does it. One message gets the stages, the names and frequently the rubric.
2. **The posting page.** The common applicant tracking systems list the stages on the public page.
3. **The reviews site**, where candidates describe the process. It blocks automated clients, so it becomes a request to the person, handled the way `method/funnel/_refs/sources.md` handles every blocked source.
4. **The name of the event in the calendar invitation.** A title carries more than it looks like it does, and a duration carries almost as much.

When nothing answers, assume the default process below and write in the dossier that the shape is presumed. A declared presumption is a thing the person can correct in the first two minutes of the call. An undeclared one is a preparation for a stage that is not happening.

## The interview methods, and what each changes

| Method | What changes in the preparation |
|---|---|
| The default behavioral and technical mix | Nothing changes. Prepare the five basics and the map of the posting |
| The chronological career walk | Build a spine through the whole career, with the transitions explained. The room is testing coherence, so the reason for each move matters more than the work inside each role |
| A principles-based method, every answer mapped to a named value | Read the published principles and map each of the person's stories to one. Answers get chosen for which principle they demonstrate |
| A values-based method drawn from a published page | The same shape, with the page as the rubric. Quote their words for the value, then tell a story that earns it |
| A live practical exercise on the company's own product | Use the product before the call, several times, and arrive with three specific observations. The exercise measures product judgment more than it measures output |

## The default process, and the three rules across it

Six stages, each probing something different.

| Stage | What it actually probes |
|---|---|
| Recruiter screening | Eligibility, compensation range, motivation, and whether the person is coherent about why this seat |
| Hiring manager | Scope. Whether what the person has owned matches what the seat owns, and whether they think about the problem the way the team does |
| Technical exercise | Working competence in the stack, and how the person behaves when something does not work |
| System design | Judgment at scale. Trade-offs, failure modes, and what the person does with an ambiguous requirement |
| Behavioral | Collaboration, conflict, ownership and self-knowledge, measured through stories |
| Final round with a founder or an executive | Alignment, ambition and whether the person raises the bar of the team they are joining |

Three rules fall across the whole process.

**The first stage is where underselling does the most damage.** Everything downstream inherits the level set there. When two seniority levels are open for the same seat, which is common, finding out which one the person is being screened for is a question for before the call, not a discovery made during it.

**The system design stage is where the line between senior and staff gets drawn.** Elegance is not what it measures. It measures whether the person can name the trade-off they chose, say what it costs, and admit where the design breaks. Somebody who narrates a clean diagram with no failure mode in it reads as somebody who has never run one of these in production.

**The behavioral stage is prepared every time and never improvised.** People assume it runs on instinct, and it has the most predictable question set of the six. See `method/funnel/_refs/basics.md`.

## What research appears in the room

The axis is the kind of fact, not the seniority of the room.

**Business understanding appears in every stage.** What the company sells, to whom, and what changed in their world recently. This is what a good answer to "why us" is made of, and a screening call is not exempt from needing one.

**A fact about a person is never recited.** It becomes a question addressed to them. The same fact delivered as a statement lands as surveillance and the room cools.

**An unverified fact is never asserted at all.** Not softened, not hedged, not attributed to something half-remembered. Left out.

What changes with the stage is the budget, not the permission. In a short screening, one sentence of it. With a hiring manager or a founder, the full opening built out of it.

**A warning about how this rule was nearly written backwards.** It started as a note from one specific call, saying not to show research. A later session generalised that note into a rule of the method, and the rule contradicted both sources that said the opposite. A lesson from one application becomes a rule of the method only after it agrees with the sources. Until then it is a note about one call, and it gets recorded as one.

## Predicting the questions

In priority order, because the budget runs out before the list does.

1. **Feedback from the previous stage.** Whatever they flagged gets asked again, frequently in the same words.
2. **Gaps the person named themselves.** They know where they are thin. So does the reader of the resume.
3. **The posting's requirements, weighted by the words spent on each.** The ranking from `method/funnel/triage.md` is already built.
4. **The shape of the stage.** What this kind of stage always asks, from the table above.

## Inherited preparation is not a source

When a stage reuses prose from the stage before it, every inherited claim gets audited against the bank again. A sentence written by an earlier session has the status of a draft, whatever polish it has acquired since.

On 2026-08-02 a claim about the person's working hours was written into one preparation, survived four rewrites and two compliance audits, and had no source in the bank at all. Every audit examined the new prose. The line was old, and old had quietly come to mean true.

Treat an inherited paragraph as somebody else's draft. Find its source in `profile/experience/` or cut it.

## The consistency brief

A short list of the claims in the sent material most likely to be probed, each with the detail underneath it.

Nothing gets said in the room that is not on the paper, and everything on the paper has to be defensible in depth. Run the two-how test from `method/funnel/_refs/xyz.md` over each claim while building this list.

A claim that cannot survive three questions is a problem with the resume rather than a problem with the preparation, and there is still time to know that before the call. Tell the person which claim it is and what the honest version sounds like.

## The map of the posting

Built before the selling points, because the selling points are derived from it.

One row per requirement, in the posting's own words. Include the bonus requirements. A bonus requirement met is the cheapest point available in the whole room, and it is the first thing dropped when the list gets decided by feel.

| Column | What goes in it |
|---|---|
| The requirement | Their words, verbatim |
| The story | Which story from `profile/experience/` answers it |
| The number | The measured figure from `profile/experience/locks.md` |
| The verdict | Matched, gap, or framing friction |

**Framing friction is the verdict nobody looks for.** The person meets the requirement, and the way they describe themselves runs against the posting.

The canonical case. A posting says plainly that the seat is for a builder and not for a manager. The person's opening leads with having led a team. Every word of it is true, and to whoever wrote that line it reads as management. The correction is never to hide the leadership. The correction is the order: lead with what they built, and let the team appear as how they built it.

**A map where every row matched and none carried friction means you mapped what you wanted to see.** Go back and read the posting again, looking for the requirement you quietly translated into something the person already has.

## Three selling points, never four

Derive them from the map. They are the three things this room should remember an hour after the call ends.

Every prepared answer returns to one of them. That is also the mechanism that stops an answer drifting past its time, because an answer with a destination ends when it arrives.

Four selling points means none of them lands. When a fourth genuinely deserves a place, one of the first three is weaker than it looked and gets cut.

## Two card lists that are not decided case by case

**The five behavioral basics**, from `method/funnel/_refs/basics.md`. Every preparation, every stage, whatever the method and whatever the time budget. The collision rule in that file gets run over them before delivery.

**One card per weighted requirement of the posting**, grouped under their own heading so the person can sweep the whole posting during the call without hunting.

A requirement marked as matched in the map with no card behind it does not exist in the room. The map is a planning artifact and the prompter is what the person has in front of them, and a match that never became a card is a match that will be answered from memory under pressure.

## The two questions the person asks

Every stage ends with the person asking, and two of those questions are worth more than the rest because of what they extract.

**The objection question.** Whether anything in the person's background gives the interviewer pause. Asked plainly, it frequently gets a straight answer, and that answer is the single best input to the next dossier. It also lets the person answer the objection while they are still in the room.

**The high-performer question.** What separates somebody excellent in this seat from somebody merely adequate. The answer is the company's own stated rubric, in their words, and the next stage's selling points get re-anchored against it.

Both go in the prompter, written out. A question improvised at the end of a call arrives as the generic one about team culture.

## Delivery and rehearsal

`method/funnel/_refs/delivery.md` governs the two files, the prompter format and the follow-up message.

Run `method/funnel/_refs/spoken-gate.md` over every answer before the rehearsal, so the rehearsal finds the remaining problems instead of the obvious ones. The filled table goes in the dossier and never in the prompter, because table rows under a card heading render on screen in the middle of a live call.

Then rehearse out loud.

## Name every gate by its file

More than one thing here answers to "the gate". An instruction saying to run the gate, without saying which one, is an instruction that will be followed wrong.

| The gate | What it reads |
|---|---|
| `method/voice.md` | Any text a reader will read, including the follow-up message |
| `method/funnel/_refs/spoken-gate.md` | Anything the person will say out loud |
| `method/funnel/_refs/xyz.md` | Any claim carrying a number |
| `method/funnel/_refs/basics.md` | The five fixed answers and the collision rule over them |

## The post-call

Called the post-call, here and everywhere else. It has been renumbered before and the cross-references rotted in three places.

Within a day, the thank-you message. It was drafted before the call, with a slot left for the specific thing to name, so it does not depend on whatever energy is left when the call ends. Structure in `method/funnel/_refs/delivery.md`, and it goes through `method/voice.md`.

The same day, without waiting for a result, capture all of this:

- **What was asked that was not predicted.** The gap between the prediction list and the real questions is how the next prediction gets better.
- **What was answered badly.** The highest-value input to the next stage, and the hardest thing to write down while it still stings. Write it anyway, in the person's words, before the memory smooths it over.
- **What the objection question extracted.** It goes straight into the next dossier as a thing to answer.
- **What the high-performer question extracted.** The next stage's selling points get re-anchored against that rubric.
- **What was learned about them.** A vague answer to a direct question, a disorganised process, a promise that changed between two stages. This is data and not a feeling. Without it the loop only learns in one direction, and the person keeps optimising for a company that has been telling them something the whole time.
- **Any new career fact**, into `profile/experience/` under ZERO-B, in the same turn, with its number into `profile/experience/locks.md`.
- **The card**, through `tools/tracker.py`, with `situation` and `next` rewritten and `fit` untouched.

An automatic transcript is the person's memory aid and never evidence about them. No line becomes a fact, a correction or a lock without the person confirming they said it. `method/funnel/_refs/delivery.md` carries the incident behind that rule.
