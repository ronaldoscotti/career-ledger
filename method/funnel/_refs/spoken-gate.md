# spoken-gate

Written text and spoken text fail in different ways. A sentence can be clean on the page and unsayable in a room, and the person finds out while saying it.

Run this gate over anything meant to leave the person's mouth: every prompter card, the opening of a behavioral answer, the compensation lines, the questions they will ask. It is separate from `method/voice.md`, which runs over text a reader will read. A text can pass one and fail the other, and most prepared answers do.

## The rules

**One breath.** A sentence that cannot be said in one breath gets rewritten, not practised. Read it aloud. If the person has to breathe in the middle, split it at the place they breathed.

**No clause inside a clause.** Nesting works on the page because a reader can go back. A listener cannot. Take the inner clause out and make it its own sentence, even when that costs a word or two.

**Numbers in spoken form.** Write the number the way a person says it. `Around two thirds` rather than a decimal. `Four months` rather than a quarter expressed in weeks. A figure the person has to convert mid-sentence is a figure they will fumble.

**Reach the point in two sentences.** An opening that takes longer gets cut from the front, not from the back. The interviewer decides whether they are interested during those two sentences, and the person almost always buried the answer under the setup.

**Words the person would actually use.** Any word they would not say to a colleague gets replaced with one they would. This applies hardest to the words a written draft reaches for: "utilise", "subsequently", "leverage". Said out loud, they sound like somebody reading, and reading is the thing the room notices.

## What does not go through this gate

Anchors, drill notes, the map of the posting, the consistency brief, and anything in a do-not-forget list. None of it is speech. Running it through here produces polished notes nobody will say and burns the budget that belonged to the answers.

## The output

A filled table, one row per rule, reported in the dossier.

| # | Check | Yes or no |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every sentence is sayable in one breath. | |
| 2 | No clause nested inside another clause. | |
| 3 | Every number is written the way it would be spoken. | |
| 4 | No opening takes more than two sentences to reach the point. | |
| 5 | No word the person would not use in conversation. | |

The table goes in the dossier and never in the prompter. Table rows under a card heading are accumulated into the card, so a self-check table placed in the prompter appears on screen in the middle of a live call. See `method/funnel/_refs/delivery.md` for the rest of the prompter constraints.
