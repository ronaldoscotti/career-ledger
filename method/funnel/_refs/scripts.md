# scripts

Every figure in this file is a placeholder. `<target base>`, `<market range>` and the rest are substituted by the person at the moment of sending, from their own research and from `profile/PROFILE.md`. A rendered amount never lives in the method, because it is stale the week after it is written and wrong for everybody except the person it came from.

## The three-part structure

Every script below follows it, and a script that drops a part loses most of its effect.

1. **Thank them and show real interest.** One sentence, specific to something from the process. Generic gratitude reads as throat-clearing before a demand.
2. **Present the data, not the want.** What the market pays for this scope, and where the research came from. A number backed by a source is a position. A number backed by a preference is a request.
3. **Make one concrete request with a specific number in it.** One request, one number, substituted by the person. Never `is there any flexibility`, which invites the answer `not much` and ends the conversation having asked for nothing.

## The five scripts

**Asked for expectations before an offer exists.** Deflect once, and only once. A second deflection reads as evasion.

```
Thanks, I am glad this is moving. I would rather understand the scope and
the level before naming a figure, since both move the number a lot. If it
helps to have a range now, roles at this scope have been landing around
<market range> and I am comfortable working from there once we have
talked through what the seat actually owns.
```

**The counter after a written offer.** Always in writing, always within two days.

```
Thank you for this, and for how clear the process has been. I want to
accept. Looking at what this role owns and at what comparable roles are
paying, I had been working toward <target base>. If you can get the base
to <target base>, I am ready to sign this week.
```

**When the base will not move.** The base is often genuinely fixed by a band. Everything around it usually is not.

```
Understood, and thank you for being straight about it. If the base is set,
I would like to close the gap another way. A signing bonus of <signing
bonus> would bridge the first year, and I would also like a written review
at six months with <target base> as the target. Either one works for me.
```

**When the offer is declared final.** Accept the finality and ask for the cheapest thing left.

```
That is clear, and I appreciate you telling me rather than leaving it
open. I am still in. Before I sign, one last thing that costs the budget
nothing: the title. <target title> reflects what this role actually owns,
and it matters to me more than the last few points of base would have.
```

**The graceful decline.** The market is small and the same people reappear.

```
I am going to decline, and I want to be straight about why: the offer
landed below what I need to make the move, and I would rather say so than
negotiate past the point where it works for either of us. I would happily
talk again in a year. Thank you for the time your team put into this.
```

## The six things never to say

| Never | What it costs |
|---|---|
| `I need at least <number>` | Frames the request as the person's problem. The company is not solving for the person's expenses. |
| `Is there any flexibility?` | Asks for nothing and gets nothing. It hands the company the whole range to answer from. |
| `My current salary is <number>` | Anchors the offer to the past, in a market that may have moved, and in some places the question is not even legal. |
| `I have another offer`, when there is none | The bluff gets called about a third of the time, and the recovery does not exist. |
| `Whatever you think is fair` | Reads as no self-assessment, and produces the bottom of the band every time. |
| `This is my final number`, before it is | Burns the only ultimatum available, and the person cannot walk it back without losing the room. |

## The contractor arithmetic

Independent contracting has to cover everything employment covered, so the two are never compared at face value. The person computes a multiplier from their own situation and applies it to the employed figure before the comparison means anything.

The multiplier absorbs employer-side taxes, unpaid time off, unpaid sick leave, equipment and its replacement, accounting and filing, the gap between contracts, and the currency and payment risk of being paid from another country. Some are known exactly and some are estimates, and every estimate gets written down with its reasoning.

Run the arithmetic before the conversation, never during it, and record the result on the card.

## What to ask for when the base will not move

In order of how often it is granted.

1. A signing bonus. The easiest yes in the list, because it comes out of a different budget and does not touch the band.
2. A written review at six months, with the target figure named in writing. Without the figure it is a conversation, not a commitment.
3. Vacation days beyond the standard allocation.
4. An equipment budget.
5. An education or conference budget. Almost nobody asks, and it is frequently sitting unspent.
6. The title. Sometimes free to the company, and it changes a resume permanently.

## The five scripts in the person's first language

Use this section when `profile/PROFILE.md` declares the language as `pt-BR`. Instructions stay in English. Only the script text is translated, and the placeholders are identical to the ones above so the substitution is the same work.

**Asked for expectations before an offer exists.**

```
Obrigado, fico feliz que esteja avançando. Prefiro entender o escopo e o
nível antes de falar um número, porque os dois mudam bastante a conta. Se
ajudar ter uma faixa agora, posições com esse escopo têm fechado em torno
de <market range>, e trabalho a partir daí depois que conversarmos sobre
o que a posição realmente entrega.
```

**The counter after a written offer.**

```
Obrigado pela proposta e pela clareza do processo inteiro. Quero aceitar.
Olhando o que essa posição entrega e o que posições comparáveis estão
pagando, eu vinha trabalhando com <target base>. Se conseguirem levar o
fixo para <target base>, assino esta semana.
```

**When the base will not move.**

```
Entendido, e obrigado pela franqueza. Se o fixo está travado, gostaria de
fechar a diferença por outro caminho. Um bônus de contratação de <signing
bonus> cobre o primeiro ano, e também gostaria de uma revisão formal em
seis meses com <target base> como alvo, por escrito. Qualquer um dos dois
funciona para mim.
```

**When the offer is declared final.**

```
Ficou claro, e agradeço por dizer isso em vez de deixar em aberto. Sigo
interessado. Antes de assinar, um último ponto que não custa orçamento: o
título. <target title> reflete o que essa posição de fato entrega, e isso
importa mais para mim do que os últimos pontos de fixo importariam.
```

**The graceful decline.**

```
Vou recusar, e quero ser direto sobre o motivo: a proposta ficou abaixo do
que preciso para fazer a mudança, e prefiro dizer isso a negociar além do
ponto em que funciona para os dois lados. Converso de novo com prazer daqui
a um ano. Obrigado pelo tempo que o time investiu nisso.
```
