# star

The story bank is constant. A different interview method is a different projection of the same stories, never a new set of stories. When a preparation finds itself writing a story that is not in `profile/experience/`, one of two things is true: the bank is incomplete, and the fix is to collect the fact under ZERO-B in `method/rules.md` before going further, or the preparation is inventing. There is no third case, and the second one ends the session.

## The four parts and their proportions

The situation is the shortest part. Two sentences, enough for the listener to know what was at stake. Everybody over-invests here because it is the easiest part to tell.

What the person did is the longest part, and it is in the first person singular. "I decided", "I wrote", "I went to the team lead". A story told in "we" gives the room no way to score the person in front of it.

The result carries the number, and the number comes from `profile/experience/locks.md`. A result with no number is an opinion about the person's own work.

The reflection is one sentence and it is optional. What they would do differently, or what the experience changed. Cut it when it adds nothing, because a story that ends on its number ends well.

## Ten coverage themes

A bank that answers all ten covers most of what gets asked. Each theme is listed with what the room is actually measuring, which is rarely the thing the question names.

| Theme | What it is testing |
|---|---|
| Shipping under ambiguity | Whether the person can act without a spec, and what they do to reduce the unknown before committing. |
| A decision that turned out wrong | Whether they notice, how fast, and whether the story is told without a villain. |
| Influence without authority | How they build agreement when they cannot instruct, which is most of senior work. |
| Conflict with a peer | Whether disagreement stays technical, and whether the relationship survived it. |
| A system that broke in production | How they triage under pressure, what they protect first, and what changed afterwards so it did not recur. |
| A trade-off deliberately accepted | Whether they can name the cost they chose to pay, out loud, without flinching. |
| Mentoring or raising the bar | Whether their effect outlasts their own output. |
| Saying no to scope | Judgment about what not to build, and whether they can hold the line with a stakeholder. |
| A project owned end to end | Ownership past the merge: rollout, adoption, the thing that broke in week three. |
| The largest thing ever owned | The ceiling of their experience, which is what the level decision turns on. |

A theme with no story behind it is a gap in the bank, and it goes into `profile/experience/` rather than being improvised in the room.

## How, how, how

Drill every story with three consecutive questions of "how" before calling it ready. Ask them out loud, and make the person answer out loud.

The first "how" is almost always answered well. The second is where a summarized story falls apart, because summary is what the person remembers and detail is what they lived. If the third answer is still specific, with names of things and reasons for choices, the story is recalled rather than reconstructed, and it will survive an interviewer who is genuinely curious.

A story that thins out on the second question is not ready. Send the person back to the period file, or pick a different story.

## The day of

Read this in five minutes, not before.

- Three selling points, out loud, once each.
- The five behavioral basics from `method/funnel/_refs/basics.md`, one line each, enough to know which story goes where.
- The number in every story that has one. If the person cannot produce the number without the notes, the notes go on screen.
- The one story most likely to be asked, told all the way through, out loud.
- The two questions the person is going to ask them.

Nothing new gets learned on the day. Anything discovered in the last hour does not make it into the room, and trying costs the composure that would have.
