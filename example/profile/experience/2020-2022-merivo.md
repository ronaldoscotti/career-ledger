# Merivo Health, March 2020 to May 2021, and the eight months after

Walked deep on 2026-08-19. This is the job that went badly, and the story below is the one she tells when a room asks about a decision that turned out wrong.

## The context

Merivo Health ran a telehealth service in Finland and Estonia, about seventy people, eight months past a Series A when she joined. She was hired as the second data engineer to build the reporting platform, remote from Kraków, reporting to the head of engineering. Nobody reported to her.

The clinical operations team needed one number every week: how much of the clinician capacity booked was actually used, by clinic and by speciality. They were producing it by hand.

## Story, the streaming platform that should have been a batch job

**Situation.** I was hired to build the reporting platform and I was told twice in the first month that real-time clinical dashboards were coming in the next two quarters. The only thing anybody was actually asking for was a weekly utilisation figure that clinical operations was assembling by hand every Friday.

**What I did.** I designed for the roadmap instead of for the request. I put a message broker in front of the booking service, wrote a stream processor to maintain the utilisation aggregates, and had it in production in four months. I knew exactly-once delivery was the hard part and I decided I could get there with idempotent writes keyed on booking identifiers. Bookings could be amended after the fact, and an amendment arrived as a new event with the same key and a different duration, so the aggregate and the booking service disagreed whenever an amendment landed out of order. I spent the next eight months patching that. I did not go back and say the design was wrong, because I had spent four months on it and I kept believing the next fix was the last one.

**Result.** Clinical operations kept two numbers, mine and their spreadsheet, and the two disagreed most weeks. Sanne de Vries, the clinical operations lead, kept a log of reports she had to reissue: eleven of the forty-one weekly reports between September 2020 and May 2021. I left in May 2021. The platform was switched off in October and replaced by a nightly batch job that one engineer wrote in three weeks.

**Reflection.** I optimised for a requirement that lived on a roadmap and not in anybody's hands. Now the first question I ask about any design is who is asking for this in the next quarter, and I ask it out loud, to the person who would be asking.

## What she would do differently

Say it was wrong at month six, when she first knew. The eight months of patching cost more than the four months of building.

## The eight months after, June 2021 to January 2022

She left without another job. Her mother was diagnosed in July 2021 and Ada was at home in Rzeszów for most of that autumn. From October she took a contract with the Kraków municipal museum group, two days a week, cleaning up a decade of ticketing exports into something their finance team could query. That ran until January 2022 and it is the reason the gap is eight months and not four.

She states the gap plainly: "I left a job that had gone badly, my mother was ill, and I came back in January 2022. I did two days a week for a museum in the back half of it."
