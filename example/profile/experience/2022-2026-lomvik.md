# Lomvik, February 2022 to August 2026

Walked deep on 2026-08-19. Two stories here, and both of them are load bearing.

## The context

Lomvik sells subscription boxes across the German-speaking market and the Nordics. Around three hundred people when she joined, four hundred and twenty when the platform team was cut in August 2026. Headquarters in Berlin, Ada remote from Kraków for the whole four and a half years.

She joined as a data engineer on a platform team of three, and was the senior engineer on a team of six by 2024. She reported to Jonas Vogt, the platform lead, for four of the four and a half years. She never had direct reports and she ran the on-call rota from 2023, mentored two engineers through their first rotation, and was the person the growth and finance teams came to when a number looked wrong.

The platform: an event stream from the web and mobile clients, an ingestion layer, a warehouse, and a nightly aggregation that produced everything the rest of the company read in the morning.

## Story one, the nightly pipeline that finished after lunch

**Situation.** The nightly aggregation finished at 11:10 in the morning on a median day. The retention team met at nine, so every Monday decision was made on Friday's numbers, and the run failed outright about nine times a month. When I joined, the accepted explanation was that the data had simply grown and the run had grown with it.

**What I did.** I did not change anything for six weeks. I instrumented every task in the pipeline for runtime and rows touched, and I put the numbers in a notebook I shared on Fridays. Seventy percent of the runtime was in one task that re-aggregated the entire order history from the beginning of the company every single night, because the original author could not be sure amendments were bounded in time. I checked: ninety-nine point four percent of amendments landed within nine days. I replaced the full re-aggregation with an incremental merge over a fourteen-day watermark and a monthly full rebuild, and I split the pipeline so that the three tables the retention team read finished first rather than last.

I argued the watermark with Jonas twice. He wanted thirty days and I wanted fourteen, and we shipped fourteen with a monthly rebuild that catches anything late. That was the trade I chose and it costs us one expensive Sunday run a month.

**Result.** The median finish time moved from 11:10 to 04:40, taken from the scheduler's own run log over thirty days in March 2023. Run failures went from nine a month to one in the following six months, from the on-call log Jonas pulled in September 2023. Compute cost for the nightly run fell thirty-one percent, thirty-eight percent including storage, from the platform team's monthly cost report.

**Reflection.** The six weeks of instrumentation were the whole job and they were the part I nearly skipped. I shipped it as a notebook instead of as a dashboard, so when the runtime crept back up in 2025 nobody noticed for a month.

## Story two, the events the mobile client was quietly dropping

**Situation.** The growth team's funnel counted about three percent fewer completed checkouts than the order ledger did, every month, for at least a year before I looked at it. Aisha Rahman on growth reconciled the two by hand before every monthly review. Everybody assumed it was network loss on mobile and nobody owned it, because the client software development kit belonged to the mobile team and the pipeline belonged to mine.

**What I did.** I had no authority over the mobile team and I did not ask for any. I added a sequence number to the client envelope, which was a small change I could make in the shared schema, and built a gap detector over the stream that told me which events were missing rather than how many. Two weeks of data showed the loss was not spread across the network at all. It was concentrated in one retry path, on one operating system version, where a failed batch was being cleared from the local buffer before the retry confirmed.

I took that to the mobile lead as a list of session identifiers and a chart, with no recommendation attached. He found the buffer bug in a day. I did not write the fix and I did not need to.

**Result.** Measured loss went from three point one percent to zero point two percent over the quarter after the fix, from the gap detector, which stayed on as a standing monitor and is still the thing that alerts when a client release regresses. Aisha stopped reconciling by hand. At peak the stream carried around forty-one million events a day in June 2026.

**Reflection.** Bringing session identifiers instead of a theory is the only reason the mobile team looked at it in a day. The year it sat there was a year where three people each half-owned it.

## What she would do differently

Ask for the platform team's charter in writing in 2024, when the reorganisation moved two of her six people onto product work and nobody wrote down what the team was still responsible for.

## How it ended

The platform team was cut to two in August 2026 in a company-wide reduction. Ada was one of four engineers made redundant, with three months of notice paid. Jonas Vogt was also made redundant and has agreed to act as a reference.
